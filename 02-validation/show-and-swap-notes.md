## What my partner understood (in their words)
I used ChatGPT for my evaluator, as I could not stay for the full class. The following is what ChatGPT provided:

**Notes:** I immediately understood this as a rep-facing morning work queue that turns recent customer signals into proposed CRM changes, with the rep validating, rejecting, or editing them. The apparent test is whether source-grounded automation can make CRM maintenance useful enough that a rep will actually engage with it. ([Lovable][1])

1. **Core hypothesis / kill switch:** “If I show reps exactly why the system wants to change an opportunity, will they trust it enough to approve the change?” The kill switch is trust: if the rep still has to independently reconstruct the deal context before clicking Validate, the product has not removed meaningful work.

2. **Where credibility broke:** the daily-action data and the intelligence disagree. Calder has a “Security review kickoff” today while yesterday’s email says security already signed off and procurement is drafting the PO; Keystone has an ROI walkthrough scheduled even though Monday’s call says the project is dead for the year. ([Lovable][1]) That makes the product look like two independently fabricated datasets rather than one system reasoning over a shared account state.

3. **Did the data feel real?** At the individual-card level, mostly: named contacts, timestamps, source types, concrete dollar values, and before/after fields create good texture. ([Lovable][1]) At the system level, no—the evidence is suspiciously perfect, every source contains exactly the facts needed to drive a clean CRM update, and the cross-record contradictions make the whole thing feel authored for a demo rather than generated from messy operating data.

_____

## Where they got confused / pushed back

There is a clear disconnect between the data presented, in particularly between the data presented to reps for the their daily tasks (next/open actions) and the updates for the customers that they need to validate. This gave the impression that this was created for a demo (read as 'prepared to be perfect').
